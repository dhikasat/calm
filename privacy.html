Privacy Policy

OmniFlow for iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch · Last updated 6 October 2026

OmniFlow does not collect your data. There is no account to create, no server to sign in to, and no analytics. Everything you write, schedule, or photograph stays in the app's own storage on your iPhone or iPad — and, only if you turn on iCloud sync, in your own private iCloud. The app works exactly the same in Airplane Mode.

What the app stores, and where

All of the following is kept in the app's private storage on your device:

  • Journal entries, including their date, mood, text, and any photo you attach.
  • Schedules and reminders you create, including their title, notes, and time.
  • Your name, location, profile photo, and daily mantras.
  • Focus timer settings and the state of a session in progress.
  • On Apple Watch, a copy of today's agenda and the day's mantra.

Some of these are stored in a shared app group so that the OmniFlow widget and Control Center control can display them. That storage is still on your device and is readable only by OmniFlow and its own widget.

iCloud sync (optional, off by default)

If you turn on Settings → iCloud → Sync with iCloud, your journal entries, photos, schedules, and focus history are also stored in the private iCloud database of your own Apple Account, so your iPhone and iPad show the same things. Apple runs that storage under its own privacy policy. It is tied to your Apple Account, not to us: the developer of OmniFlow has no server, no access to your iCloud, and no way to read, copy, or restore what is kept there.

Your name, profile photo, mantras, and settings are not synced; they stay on each device. Turning the switch off stops syncing on that device and keeps everything already on it.

Apple Watch

If you use the OmniFlow app on Apple Watch, your watch keeps its own copy of today's agenda, your name, and the day's mantra, so it can still show them when your iPhone is not nearby. Moods you log and items you tick on the watch are sent back to your iPhone and saved there.

All of that travels directly between your iPhone and your Apple Watch over Apple's own device-to-device link. It does not pass through a server, and it is never sent to us or to anyone else. Removing the app from your Apple Watch deletes the watch's copy.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Calendar. With your permission, OmniFlow shows your Apple Calendar events beside your own agenda, and writes the agendas you create into your calendar so they appear in other apps. This happens entirely through Apple's EventKit on your device. Your calendar is never sent anywhere by OmniFlow.
  • Reminders. Only if you choose it, reminders you create in OmniFlow are copied into Apple Reminders, so they also reach your Mac and Siri. OmniFlow writes and removes only its own items and never reads your other lists.
  • Health. Only if you turn on Settings → Health, the mood you choose for a new journal entry is saved to Apple Health as a State of Mind sample. OmniFlow asks for permission to write that one type of data and never reads anything from Health. Your written journal stays in the app. Health data is never used for advertising, never shared with anyone, and never leaves your device through OmniFlow.
  • Face ID or Touch ID. Only if you turn on the journal lock, to unlock the Journal tab. The check is done by iOS; OmniFlow never sees your face or fingerprint data.
  • Photos. When you attach a photo to a journal entry or set a profile picture, OmniFlow copies that single image into its own storage. It never reads your photo library otherwise.
  • Notifications. Reminders and focus-session alerts are scheduled locally by iOS. OmniFlow never sends you push notifications, and no notification content leaves your device. If iCloud sync is on, Apple's iCloud sends the app a silent signal when there is something new to fetch; it shows nothing on screen and carries none of your content.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, crash reporting, or usage measurement.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party SDKs of any kind.
  • No tracking, and no sharing of data with any other company.
  • No network requests of its own, and no server component. The only connection the app ever makes is the optional iCloud sync, which goes to Apple's iCloud under your Apple Account.

Backups you create yourself

OmniFlow can export your data to a backup file containing your entries, schedules, photos, and preferences. That file is created on your device and goes wherever you choose to send it — Files, AirDrop, email, or a cloud service of your own. Once you share it, it is governed by the terms of whatever service you sent it to, not by this policy. Keep it somewhere you trust.

Deleting your data

Settings → Data Management → Erase All Data permanently removes every journal entry, schedule, and stored photo from the app. Deleting the app from your iPhone or iPad removes everything on that device as well, and removing it from your Apple Watch removes the watch's copy.

If iCloud sync is on, Erase All Data also removes your data from iCloud and from your other synced devices. You can also delete OmniFlow's iCloud data yourself in the Settings app, under your Apple Account → iCloud → Manage Storage. Because nothing is stored on any server of ours, there is no copy for anyone to request or delete on your behalf.

Children

OmniFlow is not directed at children under 13 and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app changes what it stores or which permissions it asks for, this page will be updated and the date at the top will change.
